# Ideal customer profile

## Must-have

- Discrete manufacturing (stamping, machining, assembly, packaging machinery, electronics).
- 200–2,000 employees at the plant or the operating company.
- A CMMS already in daily use.
- A named operations or maintenance lead who owns downtime, not a CIO who owns a reliability programme.
- DACH, Nordics or Benelux.

## Disqualifying

- Process industry as the primary operation. [[06-decisions/d-008-discrete|D-008]], E-012.
- No CMMS — Pulse does not replace one, and the workflow assumes work orders have a home.
- IT as the only interested party. E-004: 7 of 9 lost deals in H1 were IT-led after an incumbent platform was already purchased.
- Plants under 80 employees. Not enough machines for the list to matter; not enough budget.

## Buying committee

| Role | Role in the deal | What they need to believe |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance / ops lead | Champion, user | The machine list is true often enough to start the shift with it |
| Plant manager | Economic buyer on mid-market deals | Downtime hours go down without a six-month project |
| IT / OT | Gate, not buyer | It does not become another platform they have to run |
| Finance | Late | Payback inside a year on one avoided line-stop |
